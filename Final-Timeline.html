CASE FILE: FULL RECORD

Every documented collapse, in order.

Fifteen losses. Four different play-callers. One recurring failure mode. Here's the record, game by game.

2019

Fiesta Bowl vs. Clemson L, 29–23

Day's first loss as full-time head coach. Ohio State was in position to win late in this College Football Playoff semifinal before a series of red-zone failures and a game-sealing interception ended the season.

Day has said it is "hard to let go" of this loss.
2020

CFP National Championship vs. Alabama L, 52–24

In a season shortened by COVID-19, Ohio State reached the national title game and was beaten decisively by top-ranked Alabama.

2021

vs. Oregon (home) L, 35–28

No. 3 Ohio State lost at home to No. 12 Oregon in September, an early sign of the vulnerability against physical, well-coached opponents that would define several future losses.

2021

vs. Michigan L, 42–27

Michigan's run game took over in the second half, ending Ohio State's eight-game winning streak in the rivalry.

Day himself called this "one bad half," specifically the second half of this game.
2022

Peach Bowl (CFP Semifinal) vs. Georgia L, 42–41

Ohio State led by 14 points and let it slip away. The most damaging sequence: getting the ball back with 51 seconds left needing only a field goal to win, and missing a 50-yard attempt after mismanaging the clock on the final drive.

Widely cited as one of the two losses that haunts Day most, alongside the 2019 Clemson game.
2022

vs. Michigan (home) L, 45–23

Second straight loss to the Wolverines. It was Michigan's first win in Columbus since 2000.

2023

at Notre Dame L, 17–14

Ohio State lost a three-point game on the road to Notre Dame on a last-second field goal.

2023

vs. Michigan L, 30–24

A third consecutive loss to Michigan, extending the losing streak and intensifying outside pressure on Day, including public criticism from analysts questioning his job security.

2024

vs. Oregon L, 32–31

Ohio State had a fourth-and-short chance to seal the win in Eugene. An offensive pass interference call on the final possession helped end the Buckeyes' bid, and the clock ran out before a final play could be run.

Chip Kelly was OC/play-caller this season. Ohio State's only loss besides Michigan came here.
2024

vs. Michigan (home) L, 13–10

A fourth straight loss to Michigan, particularly stinging as a home loss to an unranked Michigan team. Widely described as one of the worst losses of the Day era, though Ohio State still made and won the expanded 12-team playoff weeks later.

2024

CFP National Championship vs. Notre Dame W, 34–23

The one full season without a signature collapse. Chip Kelly called plays independently all year. Ohio State finished 14–2 and won its first national title since 2014.

2025

Big Ten Championship vs. Indiana L, 13–10

Ohio State was undefeated (12-0) entering the game. Shut out in the second half of the one game that mattered most, with Brian Hartline as primary play-caller.

2025

Cotton Bowl (CFP Quarterfinal) vs. Miami L, 24–14

Just weeks after taking play-calling duties back from Hartline, Day called this game himself. Ohio State's season ended in the quarterfinals.

2026

vs. Texas L, 24–23

Led 23–3 with 8:09 left in the third quarter, a win probability north of 98%. Gave up 21 unanswered points. The first loss in program history after leading by 17+ points at halftime. New OC Arthur Smith's first marquee test as play-caller.

Center Carson Hinzman, postgame: "We really just beat ourselves."
2026

at USC L, 30–18

Ohio State never led against an unranked USC team. The defense gave up a 69-yard touchdown run on the second snap and a 70-yard reception, and 30 points in all, the most it had allowed in 31 games. The offensive line was pressured 21 times and flagged four times. The second loss before November in over ten years.

2026

vs. No. 23 Oregon L, 41–28

Ohio State went up 14–0 at home and lost by 13, giving up 41 points to Oregon. A third loss in 2026 and the seventh time the Day era has lost a game after holding a lead.

Across four different play-callers, the pattern holds.

Build a big lead through aggressive offense. Get conservative on both sides of the ball while the opponent has nothing left to lose. The one season this didn't happen, 2024 under an independent, experienced coordinator, ended in a national championship. That is not a coincidence.
