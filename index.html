CASE FILE: 7 YEARS, 15 LOSSES

Seven years. Fifteen losses. One pattern.

Ohio State has the talent to win every game on its schedule. Under Ryan Day, it keeps losing the same way, in the same moments, against the same caliber of opponent. The record is documented. Ryan Day should be fired, and at the very least, the staff he hired has to go.

THE ONE-YEAR EXCEPTION

Four play-callers. One year without a collapse.

In 2024, Ryan Day handed play-calling to Chip Kelly, an experienced coordinator with full autonomy. It's the only season in the Day era that didn't end in a signature, program-defining collapse.

THE HIRES THAT MADE IT WORSE

Three hires. The same problem.

An offensive coordinator with a history of collapse, a defensive coordinator whose unit breaks down away from home, and an offensive line that struggles in the biggest games. Every one was Ryan Day's call.

FALCONS HEAD COACH, 2021–2023

Fired after three straight losing seasons.

Arthur Smith's only head coaching job ended in Atlanta. Three years, three 7–10 seasons, zero playoff appearances. His own late-season play-calling was singled out as part of the problem.

WHAT WE'RE ASKING FOR

This isn't about anger. It's about accountability.

We're asking the administration to act on seven years of documented evidence. Our position is simple: fire Ryan Day. If the administration won't go that far, the minimum acceptable response is to fire the staff responsible for these results.

FIRE THE HEAD COACH

A coaching change.

Ohio State moves on from Ryan Day as head coach and conducts a genuine external search for a replacement who can close out the games this program keeps losing.

AT THE VERY LEAST, FIRE HIS STAFF

Clean out the coaches responsible.

If Day stays, the coaches he hired must go: offensive coordinator Arthur Smith, defensive coordinator Matt Patricia and offensive line coach Tyler Bowen, along with anyone else the administration finds responsible for these results. Keeping the head coach while keeping the same staff is not accountability.
