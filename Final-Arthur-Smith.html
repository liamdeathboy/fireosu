CASE FILE — THE HIRES

Three hires. The same problem.

An offensive coordinator with a record of collapse. A defensive coordinator whose unit has come apart away from home. An offensive line that keeps breaking down in the biggest games. Every one of them was a choice Ryan Day made.

01 — OFFENSIVE COORDINATOR

Arthur Smith: a résumé built on the exact problem he was hired to fix.

After back-to-back late collapses closed out 2025, Ohio State needed discipline and aggression in big games. In January 2026, Day hired Arthur Smith. His first marquee game as coordinator was a blown 23-3 lead at Texas.

Atlanta Falcons

Head Coach · 2021–2023

21-30. Three straight 7-10 seasons. Zero playoff appearances. Arthur Smith's only experience as a head coach ended in Atlanta after three years without a winning record. Falcons owner Arthur Blank cited results that "have not met our expectations" when announcing the firing.

It wasn't just the record — it was how the losses happened. In Week 15 of his final season, with playoff positioning on the line, ESPN specifically called Smith's play-calling "suspect," pointing to an overly conservative game plan against a one-win Carolina team that helped cost Atlanta the game. Atlanta also posted a specific pattern across Smith's tenure: reaching .500 mid-season and then collapsing down the stretch, in both 2021 and 2022.

Pittsburgh Steelers

Offensive Coordinator · 2024–2025

Two years. Bottom-half production both years. Smith's offense ranked 24th in the NFL in yards per game and 16th in points per game, on average, across his two seasons. By advanced metrics it was similarly unremarkable — 18th in DVOA, 19th in EPA per play.

In 2025, despite Pittsburgh acquiring a future Hall of Fame quarterback (Aaron Rodgers) and adding a difference-making receiver (DK Metcalf), the passing offense did not improve — Pittsburgh actually finished 22nd in the league in passing yards, and yards per attempt went down from the year before. When Smith left Pittsburgh, the fan reaction was not nostalgic. It was relief.

Ohio State

Offensive Coordinator · 2026–present

Zero college coaching experience before this hire. Arthur Smith had never coached a single down of college football before being handed the offense of one of the most talented rosters in the country — tasked specifically with fixing a program-wide pattern of late-game collapses.

His first true test came at Texas. Ohio State led 23-3 with 8:09 left in the third quarter — ESPN gave the Buckeyes a win probability north of 98% entering the fourth. Ohio State scored 3 points in the entire second half. Texas scored 21 unanswered. Final: Texas 24, Ohio State 23 — the first time in program history Ohio State lost after leading by 17+ points at halftime.

It wasn't a one-off. Ohio State then failed to ever lead at USC (30–18), and on November 7 went up 14–0 on No. 23 Oregon at home before losing 41–28.

02 — DEFENSIVE COORDINATOR

Matt Patricia: five losses in two seasons, and the big ones come away from home.

Ohio State has lost five games since Patricia arrived in 2025. Four of them were at neutral sites or on the road. Four of the five came against ranked opponents. The fifth was an unranked USC team that scored 30. A week later, Oregon scored 41 in Columbus.

Ohio State

Defensive Coordinator · 2025–present

The pattern is where the defense breaks. At Texas, after Ohio State went up 23-3, the Longhorns ran 40 plays across three straight touchdown drives. They gained 239 yards at 5.9 per play, and the defense recorded exactly one tackle for loss. At USC, the defense gave up a 69-yard touchdown run on the second snap of the game and 30 points in all, the most it had allowed in 31 games, before Oregon put up 41 on it at home.

In both 2025 losses on neutral fields, the defense could not get the late stop it needed against a ranked opponent. In 2026 Ohio State is 2–2 against ranked teams, and the two losses allowed 24 at Texas and 41 to Oregon.

The fair context: Patricia's 2025 unit was one of the best in the country through the regular season. The case isn't that he can't coach defense. It's that the defense comes apart in exactly the games that decide seasons, and that it can't get the stop when a lead needs closing.
03 — OFFENSIVE LINE

Tyler Bowen: a line that breaks down in big games and is never fully healthy.

Justin Frye left after 2024. Ryan Day hired Tyler Bowen in February 2025 specifically to fix Ohio State's offensive line, both on the field and on the recruiting trail. Two seasons in, the line has been the weak link when the games matter most.

2025

Bowen's first season

Six sacks allowed in the whole regular season. Ten in the last two games. The line was dominant through the regular season, then gave up five sacks to Indiana in the Big Ten title game and five more to Miami in the Cotton Bowl — 71 yards lost — in the two games that ended the season. Right guard Tegra Tshabola, who had surgery between the two games, was blamed for much of it, but the problems were not limited to one player.

2026

Bowen's second season

Four starters back, expected to be a strength, and it isn't. Right tackle Phillip Daniels was hurt in the Texas game. Captain Austin Siereveld has been moved around and missed the Maryland game, and the line was missing starters for five straight games before USC. Against Indiana the line allowed four sacks for 44 yards. At USC it was pressured 21 times and flagged repeatedly (four penalties from the line).

The line has been repeatedly short-handed, though nobody can prove the cause: Reporters covering the team now describe Bowen as being on the hot seat.

Recruiting

The long-running gap

The offensive line has been Ohio State's weakest recruiting position for years. The sharpest criticism landed on Frye, whose first two recruiting cycles landed only three out-of-state linemen. Bowen was brought in to end that. His first classes have drawn credit for four-star depth, but he has yet to land a five-star lineman, and the starting group is still largely made up of players Frye recruited and developed. The question in year two is no longer recruiting. It's whether Bowen can turn that talent into a line that holds up in November and January.

Three hires. Every one of them Ryan Day's call.

Ryan Day picked the play-caller, the defensive coordinator and the line coach. He had seven years of his own program's history to study. Each hire had a track record that pointed straight at the problem he needed to solve, and each has shown up in the same big games.
