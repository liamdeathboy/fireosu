THE OBVIOUS OBJECTION

"Isn't an $80.5 million buyout too expensive to justify?"

The honest answer

This is the most legitimate pushback to this entire case, and it deserves a real answer instead of being ignored.

The $80.5 million figure is the maximum exposure, reduced by an offset if Day is hired elsewhere. The actual net cost would likely be lower. It's also typically structured as payments over time, not a single lump-sum hit to next year's budget. Set against $336.1 million in annual athletic department revenue, a one-time buyout, even a large one, is a fraction of a single year's operating budget, not an existential financial threat.

Programs across the country have paid large buyouts to move on from coaches who won plenty of games but couldn't win the ones that mattered most, and the return, when it works, is measured in tens of millions in additional playoff revenue, donor giving, and brand value. Ohio State's own record revenue year followed directly from a national championship season.

The actual question isn't "can Ohio State afford to make a change." At $336 million in annual revenue, they can. The real question is whether the pattern is bad enough, and consistent enough, to justify paying it. We think seven years of the same failure, at the highest levels of competition, clears that bar.

Nearly half of Day's losses have come by blowing a lead he already had.

Ohio State doesn't need to take our word for the pattern. The numbers file it themselves.
